# Desk 42 — System Constitution

> Status: corrected design constitution. This defines the game’s promises and boundaries; it is not an implementation plan.

## 1. Whole-game thesis

> **Desk 42 is a darkly comic alien society simulation where the player never learns what truly happened. They decide what officially counts, and people, institutions and anomalies reorganise around each ruling until their adaptations become the next case.**

Internal axiom:

> **Reality is simulated. Official reality is computed. The player can act only on evidence.**

The player does not directly command the society. Their sovereign power is institutional recognition: deciding which identities, relationships, injuries, ownership claims, obligations and liabilities the Branch will recognise and enforce.

## 2. The recursive lock

The game exists only when this complete loop operates:

```text
Lived incident
→ agent belief, memory and action
→ surviving evidence
→ case
→ ruling
→ official rights, status and resources change
→ agents and organisations adapt
→ new lived incidents, evidence and cases
```

Three elements are inseparable:

1. **Executable rulings** change material conditions, not merely text or scores.
2. **Persistent agents adapt** to the institution according to their beliefs, relationships and incentives.
3. **Adaptations generate future cases** through traceable lived consequences.

If a feature does not strengthen this loop, it is not part of the core game. If any one of the three elements is absent, Desk 42 becomes a conventional desk game, policy simulator or character simulation.

## 3. The ruling contract

A ruling is not a binary button. Every completed ruling contains five distinct parts:

| Part | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Finding** | The proposition the institution accepts, rejects or leaves unresolved. |
| **Disposition** | Approve, deny, hold, provisionally approve, escalate or dismiss. |
| **Remedy** | The concrete response: payment, recognition, protection, sanction, recovery, correction or none. |
| **Holding** | The institutional reason connecting the accepted evidence to the disposition. |
| **Scope** | The people, organisations, incident types, jurisdictions and time periods to which that reasoning may apply. |

Two cases can reach the same disposition through different holdings and therefore create different futures. The player’s creative power lies in choosing not only **what happens now**, but **what the institution has agreed is true and why**.

### Local rulings and cited precedent

- Every ruling changes the current case’s official state and produces immediate material consequences.
- A holding remains local unless it is cited as authority by a later ruling, policy or delegated procedure.
- Citation makes a holding operational for its stated scope; it does not make every past ruling globally binding by default.
- A cited holding can be narrowed, distinguished, superseded or retired, but doing so changes which cases can rely on it and which previous decisions remain defensible.
- An appeal contests the finding, procedure, holding or scope. It is not merely a second accuracy check.

### Scope is a frequent build decision

Scope is not reserved for rare constitutional moments. Every consequential ruling ends with a holding-and-scope clause. Narrowing, extending or distinguishing precedent is therefore one of the player’s recurring build edits, made case by case rather than only in a separate policy menu.

- **Narrow scope** limits exposure and exploitation but preserves repeated uncertainty and manual work.
- **Broad scope** creates leverage, consistency and delegation while opening larger loopholes and wider appeal consequences.
- **Retrospective scope** can repair old exclusions while reopening settled obligations.

The game must repeatedly ask: **How much of society should this reason govern?**

## 4. The observation contract

The player never receives authoritative lived truth.

They may observe:

- surviving records and physical evidence;
- testimony, allegations, retractions and silence;
- official histories and policy versions;
- visible agent behaviour;
- payments, sanctions, protections and status changes;
- later claims, appeals and social consequences.

They may not inspect:

- a ground-truth flag;
- an objectively correct verdict;
- an agent’s exact private beliefs or motives;
- hidden utility scores or relationship numbers;
- a post-case revelation that resolves every uncertainty.

Explainability is procedural rather than omniscient. The game can show what evidence entered the file, which requests returned nothing or remain unresolved, which policy and citations were used, and why the official result followed. It may not identify undiscovered evidence as “missing”; an absence becomes visible only when the institution has an observable reason to notice it. The game can show an agent’s stated reasons and observable history, but not certify those reasons as complete or true.

Consequences reveal **what the ruling caused**, not **what really happened**. A coherent false belief may produce the same visible action as a true one. The mystery is epistemic; the causality is not arbitrary.

## 5. The agent contract

Desk 42 is built around a small population of persistent, named agents rather than anonymous claim volume.

Each agent has five functional layers:

1. **Needs** — health, subsistence, safety, belonging and autonomy.
2. **Commitments** — dependants, employment, debt, promises, duties and organisational loyalty.
3. **Relationships** — trust, fear, obligation, authority and attachment.
4. **Beliefs and memories** — propositions with a source, confidence, timestamp, secrecy and record status.
5. **Institutional status** — the identities, rights, protections, liabilities and restrictions the Branch recognises.

Institutional standing is an affordance: it determines which procedures, remedies and protections an agent may access. Institutional trust is an attitude held by an agent toward an institution. Neither is a universal biological or psychological need, and neither may be collapsed into a single loyalty score.

Agents act only on information available to them. They may be mistaken, deceptive or internally conflicted. They become memorable through persistent state and consequences, not bespoke quest scripts or omniscient behaviour.

Presentation identity is not a causal variable. Substituting an agent with the same decision-relevant needs, commitments, relationships, beliefs, available information and institutional standing should preserve the causal pattern. Divergence is expected only when the substitution changes a load-bearing factor actually read by the relevant decision; changing an unrelated belief or cosmetic identity must not manufacture a different story.

## 6. The systemic action contract

Every consequential agent or institutional action must satisfy the following:

1. It has an identifiable actor or authority.
2. It follows from available knowledge, pressure, relationships or institutional rules.
3. It changes material, social or official state.
4. It can leave records, memories, witnesses, absence or other evidence.
5. It creates or closes at least one future possibility for another action, case or appeal.

An event that only produces flavour text or moves an unexplained global meter is not systemic content.

Cases must originate in lived incidents. Appeals and descendant cases must arise from prior rulings, evidence changes or agent reactions. The Director may expose a chain; it may not substitute for one.

## 7. The bounded anomaly contract

Anomalies exist to stress the society’s categories, not to introduce a separate combat game or arbitrary magic.

- Each anomaly introduces a small, legible exception concerning identity, embodiment, time, ownership, memory, dependency, evidence or causality.
- Its exception is stable enough for agents and institutions to form expectations around it.
- Institutional recognition may physically or socially constrain anomalous behaviour, but only through established, inspectable rules.
- Anomalous consequences must follow from state and precedent; they may not appear as unexplained punishment, random spectacle or authorial rescue.
- The human consequence remains understandable even when the underlying fact is impossible.

The old Fugue mode, action layer and sanity escalation are excluded. Its valuable idea survives only as ordinary systemic causality: in this world, an official classification can sometimes make an anomalous condition materially enforceable.

## 8. The Director contract

The Case Director controls pacing and attention, not narrative intelligence.

It may:

- monitor a small set of pressure signals;
- select an eligible incident category;
- choose eligible participants from the existing population;
- foreground an unresolved consequence when pacing requires it.

It may not:

- read the player’s build and invent a bespoke counter;
- identify and exploit loopholes on an agent’s behalf;
- manufacture irony or multi-stage story arcs;
- override knowledge, relationships or institutional state;
- create an outcome that the simulation could not otherwise produce.

The same incident must still diverge under different institutions when Director intelligence is removed or replaced by a fixed schedule. The Director presents pressure; the society generates the story.

## 9. Reality Debt is excluded

Reality Debt is not a currency, meter, debuff, difficulty scalar or source of random chaos.

Contradictions exist locally as concrete conflicts between findings, classifications, rights and anomalous rules. Their consequences belong to the affected people, organisations, cases and precedents. They are not converted into a universal number the player accumulates or spends.

The phrase may survive as diegetic bureaucratic language, but it has no authority over the simulation. There is no separate Fugue threshold at which arbitrary effects begin.

## 10. Institutional builds

A build is a coherent but exploitable institutional regime, not a throughput layout or stack of flat bonuses.

It is composed from five interacting layers:

1. **Doctrine** — what counts as sufficient proof and where doubt falls.
2. **Procedure** — how evidence is requested, shared, protected, sealed or challenged.
3. **Delegation** — which findings and remedies staff may issue without direct intervention.
4. **Cited precedent and scope** — which holdings currently carry force and how broadly they apply.
5. **Capacity** — which forms of investigation, advocacy, identity work, household assessment and adjudication the Branch can perform.

A meaningful build changes:

- which lived events become visible to the institution;
- which people receive rights, money, protection or sanctions;
- which cases can be delegated;
- which strategies agents and organisations expect to succeed;
- which descendant cases the society produces.

Build-caused chaos must be attributable. A records-first institution may teach employers to manipulate records. Strong witness protection may uncover concealed harm while making protected status economically valuable. Broad continuity precedent may stabilise displaced workers while encouraging organisations to maintain competing identity systems.

There is no universally correct institution and no final state of perfect automation. More verification cannot purchase away normative conflict. Every strong regime creates constituencies, blind spots and exploitable definitions.

## 11. The ending contract

The ending does not reveal lived truth, calculate a morally correct score or announce how many rulings were objectively right.

For the final review, the player freezes the Branch’s charter, procedures and cited precedents, appoints its continuing decision-makers, and observes a fixed period without further constitutional redesign. The institution continues exercising judgement; the player has not eliminated it through automation.

The conclusion may report only observable or institutional outcomes: solvency, delays, appeals, recorded household stability, public claims, employer concentration, unresolved contradictions, visible departures and the later lives of known agents. Legal, workers’ organisations, employers, claimants and oversight bodies issue competing assessments from the evidence and interests available to them. None is the designer’s hidden answer, and disagreement between them is not collapsed into a final score.

The final question is not **Did you discover the truth?** It is **What kind of institution will continue deciding without you?**

## 12. Proof conditions

The design thesis is supported only if all of the following are true:

1. The same seed and initial incident produce materially different histories under different institutional builds.
2. Divergence survives a fixed incident schedule and does not depend on an adversarial Director.
3. Consequential agent actions arise from shared systemic rules rather than character-specific branches.
4. Those actions create new evidence, cases, appeals or changes in institutional behaviour.
5. Every major consequence can be traced through observable procedure and prior state without exposing authoritative truth.
6. Identical dispositions reached through different holdings or scopes create different downstream effects.
7. At least one frequent scope decision benefits one named agent or group while destabilising another.
8. At least one bounded anomaly creates a physical consequence from an established institutional classification without invoking a separate mode or arbitrary event.
9. Replacing an agent with an equivalent decision-relevant profile — including available information and institutional standing — preserves the causal pattern despite different presentation identity.
10. Replacing that agent with a profile that differs on a load-bearing factor read by the relevant decision can change the first affected outcome for a traceable reason; material difference elsewhere is not sufficient.
11. A belief perturbation is expected to flip behaviour only when the changed proposition is available to the agent and load-bearing in that choice. Perturbing an unrelated or unread belief must not create a false divergence.
12. At least one consequence of an early ruling produces a separate, later incident involving the same persistent population rather than a scripted continuation of the original case.

## 13. Kill conditions

The direction must be rejected or materially redesigned if:

- believable stories require bespoke character scripts;
- the Director must invent ironic counters to make the society react;
- rulings remain approve/deny outcomes without meaningful findings, holdings and scope;
- policies mainly change aggregate metrics or processing speed;
- players can solve uncertainty by purchasing enough verification;
- agent behaviour is only understandable through hidden-state readouts;
- consequences cannot be attributed to earlier evidence, rulings or relationships;
- profiles that differ on a load-bearing decision factor remain behaviourally interchangeable, or named agents differ only through presentation;
- anomalies are cosmetic flavour or sources of arbitrary chaos;
- the game becomes interesting only when a designer explains the simulation afterward.

## 14. Current-stage product wording

Use wording that describes the product and the design hypothesis without claiming unproven technology:

> **Dark Lattice Ltd is developing Desk 42, a systemic alien society simulation designed around governing through case rulings. Its current product hypothesis is that executable institutional rules can cause persistent agents to adapt and generate future disputes. Its planned in-house Institutional Reality system is intended to separate lived events from official recognition while preserving evidence provenance and non-omniscient, traceable consequences.**

“Institutional Reality Engine” may be used as the internal name for this architecture. At the current stage it must not be presented as finished middleware, a proven proprietary platform or an intelligent AI storyteller. Executable rules, agent adaptation and descendant disputes remain design claims until the proof conditions above are demonstrated. Desk 42 is the product; the architecture is the means being developed to make its systemic claim testable.

## 15. Explicit exclusions

The current core contains no bulk claim factory, colony-survival layer, combat route, Door action game, Fugue mode, omniscient world map, universal Reality Debt meter or engine-licensing proposition.

The game remains intimate: a persistent alien micro-society, an institution with scarce attention, and rulings whose reasons return as lived consequences.
