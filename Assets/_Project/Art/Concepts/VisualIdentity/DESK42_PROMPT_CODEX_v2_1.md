# Desk 42 — Production Art Direction and Prompt Codex v2.1

**Status:** verification draft; no generation or Unity implementation is authorised until this document is approved.

**Purpose:** lock one coherent playable office composition, its claimant presentation, its pixel language, and its sanity-state mutations before further ComfyUI work or production redraws.

This document supersedes Prompt Codex v2 wherever the two conflict. It does not replace `DESK42_PIXEL_ART_IDENTITY.md`; that document remains the visual-identity authority.

## 1. Non-negotiable identity

Desk 42 is a deadpan 1990s claims office in which mundane bureaucracy contains impossible clients and events. The ordinary office must be attractive, useful, and immediately legible before anything supernatural occurs.

Every image must preserve the four pillars:

1. **Analog authority** — cream laminate, dark enamel, Bakelite, chrome, CRT glass, punched paper, stamps, mechanical drawers, and brass task lighting.
2. **Paper is the world** — forms communicate work state, hierarchy, progress, mistakes, and danger.
3. **Controlled intrusion** — anomaly colour and impossible matter are rationed and mechanically meaningful.
4. **Hostile geometry** — low sanity corrupts a known composition; it does not replace the office with random horror decoration.

The governing horror rule is:

> The room is ordinary first. One thing is unmistakably wrong second.

## 3. The locked gameplay composition

### 3.1 Camera

- Native gameplay reference canvas: **384 × 216**, 16:9.
- Fixed **3/4 orthographic office view**, not a generic isometric dollhouse.
- The camera is on the claims worker's side of the desk, looking toward the claimant.
- The player chair may enter the lower foreground as a cropped anchor, but the player is never shown as a separate character.
- The claimant sits upper-centre, physically across the desk, facing the player.
- The room, desk, claimant footprint, and camera do not move between sanity tiers.
- No Dutch angle, lens distortion, perspective drift, or camera change is used to manufacture horror.

### 3.2 One desk, not two

- The stage contains **one continuous claims desk** with one dominant horizontal silhouette.
- A shallow player-side cut-out is allowed, but it must read as part of the same slab.
- Do not describe the desk as L-shaped, U-shaped, paired, adjoining, or modular in generation prompts.
- No side table, second workstation, interview table, or duplicate desk may appear.
- The claimant and player share opposite sides of the same desk.

### 3.3 Fixed room anchors

| Anchor | Locked placement | Gameplay purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Office door | back-left wall, fully visible | claimant entry and passive-tentacle source |
| Noticeboard | left background, between door and claimant sightline | paper-world storytelling and board-bleed source |
| Claimant | upper-centre, seated across desk | social and emotional focus |
| Wall clock | upper-right wall, clearly readable | persistent animated sanity indicator |
| Filing cabinet | far-right background | storage silhouette and occupied-cabinet source |
| Fluorescent fixture | above processing axis | healthy light authority and flicker state |
| Wall/ceiling seam | visible across upper room | reserved route for the ethereal-tube anomaly |

These anchors may change state but not location. The doorway, noticeboard, cabinet, and clock must not compete with the claimant's face or hands.

### 3.4 Desk zones

| Zone | Locked placement | Contents and rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Inbox | upper-left desktop | one wire tray with incoming cream forms |
| Processing | central desktop | strongest light, current form, stamp, and clear handoff space |
| Outbox | upper-right desktop | no more than two completed folders |
| Personal | far-left corner | enamel mug plus one small non-figurative personal token |
| Dead zone | front-centre/right negative space | deliberately clear; reserved for specific anomaly events |

Supporting placement:

- CRT terminal: left of centre, low enough that it never covers the claimant's face or hands.
- Keyboard/input device: directly in front of the CRT, aligned to the player.
- Printer or claims machine: right of centre, subordinate to the current form.
- Pen holder: back-right corner.
- Crumpled paper: discard lane at front-left, maximum one hero wad in the clean state.
- Active papers: right queue lane; never spread uniformly across the desk.
- Player chair: lower-centre, partially cropped.

### 3.5 Forbidden composition errors

- No claimant portrait, framed client photograph, wall portrait, hologram, or video-call substitute.
- No empty claimant chair in a finished gameplay or presentation composite.
- No second desk or second CRT workstation.
- No claimant seated beside the desk or facing away from the player.
- No CRT, cards, or props covering the claimant's face or hands.
- No empty room used as the final playable scene.
- No anonymous clutter used merely to make the room look busy.

The personal corner may contain an enamel mug, staff badge, union pin, folded note, or small maintenance token. It must not contain a figurative photograph that can be mistaken for the current claimant.

## 4. Compositional hierarchy

The player's eye should read the scene in this order:

1. Current claimant face and silhouette.
2. Current form in the processing zone.
3. Decision-relevant desk equipment.
4. Claim queue and status props.
5. Room-state signal such as clock, light, cabinet, board, or door.
6. Background decoration.

At clean sanity, the claimant and current paper share the strongest readable contrast. At corrupted sanity, the hero anomaly may briefly challenge that hierarchy but must never hide task-critical information.

## 5. Pixel and material language

### 5.1 Construction

- Production composition is authored or redrawn on the 384 × 216 native grid.
- Review at 1× and an integer-scaled preview; never judge only from a large preview.
- Use nearest-neighbour scaling, Point filtering, and integer transforms.
- Use hard clusters, sparse purposeful dithering, and selective one-native-pixel outlines.
- Use two shadow steps: form shadow and contact shadow.
- Avoid single-pixel confetti except for eyes, dust, sparks, and one-frame rupture events.
- Do not use soft gradients, bloom, antialiasing, depth of field, PBR reflections, or painterly edge noise.

“16-bit adventure game” is mood shorthand, not a construction rule. Native grid, palette, clusters, silhouette, and state consistency are the construction rules.

### 5.2 Material shorthand

| Material | Pixel treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Bakelite | rounded dark mass, one tight cream specular, minimal internal texture |
| Cream enamel/laminate | broad flat plane, warm chipped edge, one controlled shadow ramp |
| Chrome | short stepped cool band, no soft reflection |
| Paper | cream field, ruled clusters, one shadow ramp, sparse fibre only at close scale |
| Brass | brown base, warm midtone, restrained amber highlight |
| CRT glass | near-black green field, block reflection or scan band, no bloom |
| Linoleum | broad tiles with sparse wear; grid remains subordinate |
| Anomaly matter | hard-edged or deliberately stepped; never generic translucent VFX fog |

### 5.3 Palette discipline

Healthy scenes follow the visual-identity palette and a rough **70 / 20 / 8 / 2** distribution:

- 70% institutional structure: deep green and paper cream.
- 20% warm grey, dusty blue-grey, brown, and supporting neutral ramps.
- 8% task accents: muted orange and approval red.
- At most 2% anomaly accents: teal or electric blue.

Glitch magenta is absent before the Impossible tier and appears only as a tiny rupture cluster thereafter.

Hex values guide cleanup and palette transfer; they are not expected to be obeyed by a text-to-image model.

## 6. Lighting contract

- Clean default: one upper-left fluorescent authority light with stable, short contact shadows.
- CRT emission is allowed as a hard one- or two-pixel green rim; no bloom.
- Night scenes may explicitly override the fluorescent with the brass desk lamp.
- Anomalous light may explicitly override local pixels only; it does not wash the whole room in neon.
- Every prompt must state whether it uses `clean fluorescent`, `warm task lamp`, or `anomaly override` lighting.
- When two light temperatures coexist, their edges remain stepped and local.

The phrase “no glow” means no soft bloom or gradient halo. It does not forbid a CRT screen or anomaly from emitting a controlled hard-edged light band.

## 7. Claimant contract

### 7.1 Presentation

- Claimants are physical visitors seated across the desk.
- All species occupy the same upper-centre footprint and share eye line, desk edge, light direction, and approximate hand-rest position.
- The claimant must read clearly at native resolution through silhouette before facial detail.
- Sober office clothing grounds the impossible anatomy.
- Each species receives one silhouette hook, one material hook, and one behavioural hook.
- Do not turn every species into a human with cosmetic prosthetics.
- Do not turn every anomaly into a face covered with extra eyes or teeth.

### 7.2 Core species

| Runtime ID | Silhouette hook | Material hook | Behaviour hook |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `moth_accountant` | antennae and folded wing mass | dusty scales and wool suit | grooms antennae, worries paper edges |
| `gel_anomaly` | non-human amorphous head/torso boundary | clear gel with suspended teal inclusions | bubbles, settles, briefly loses expression alignment |
| `unregistered_alien` | long narrow skull and large orbital shapes | matte olive skin with restrained moist highlights | over-still gaze, precise finger placement |
| `void_proxy` | hard mask-like head and enclosed negative space | near-black plane with teal rim discontinuities | missing-frame turns and impossible holds |

The exact anatomy is subject to artist refinement, but these hooks must remain recognisable and species-specific.

For `gel_anomaly`, “human-readable” describes emotional legibility only. The claimant has no human skin, skull, face, or person underneath the gel mass.

### 7.3 Behavioural state set

Every production claimant requires:

1. Pending
2. Agitated
3. Litigious
4. Cooperative
5. Suspicious
6. Resigned
7. Paranoid
8. Dissociating
9. Smug

Use held 6–10 fps pixel animation. A readable tell precedes the state transition. One strange movement is stronger than constant idle wobble.

The minimum verification sheet before full production is:

- All four species in Pending, seated in the shared gameplay footprint.
- One complete nine-state Moth Accountant set.
- One fidget sequence shown at 1× native scale and 4× nearest-neighbour preview.

## 8. Equipment and room-state grammar

Equipment remains mundane until a state change corrupts its established function.

| Object | Clean behaviour | Supporting unease | Allowed hero anomaly |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Clock | honest second-hand tick | hand runs too fast or shadow disagrees | detached clock or impossible hand count |
| Desk lamp | warm task pool | delayed switch response | casts a hard circle of absence |
| Filing cabinet | aligned closed drawers | one drawer open exactly three inches | occupied drawer bulges patiently |
| Noticeboard | squared pinned forms | one memo slowly rotates | dark matter seeps behind straight memos |
| Door | closed or normal claimant entry | latch moves once | passive tentacle rests through narrow gap |
| CRT | stable block cursor and form | one short sync slip | interface detaches from screen boundary |
| Forms | clean stacks and readable status blocks | one wrong-ink form | suspended papers or cropped-by-void paper |
| Telephone | ordinary ring cadence | rings once with no call | receiver shadow or cord occupies wrong position |

Only one object receives the fully committed hero anomaly in a scene. Other objects may show tier-appropriate supporting symptoms, but they cannot introduce a second creature, portal, or spectacle.

## 9. Sanity ladder

| Tier | Room treatment | Hero-anomaly allowance |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Clean `100–75` | correct geometry, stable light, clean paper, strict baselines | none |
| Uneasy `74–50` | geometry remains true; behavioural disagreement, flicker, slightly long shadows | no creature or portal; one subtle contradiction |
| Contaminated `49–25` | walls lean 2–3 degrees, paper yellows, rust and sickly green enter locally | one physical intrusion, fully readable |
| Impossible `24–1` | heavy desaturation, gaps, detached layers, teal/blue survive | one geometry-breaking hero; magenta limited to 1–3 native-pixel clusters |
| Fugue `0` | near-desaturated office; foreground and interface disagree on position | one established anomaly may bridge the disagreement |

The desk, claimant, door, board, cabinet, and clock remain identifiable at every tier.

Fugue must not generate a literal second furnished room. The teal “ghost office” is a separately controllable offset silhouette or runtime layer derived from the same composition.

## 10. Hero anomaly library

Use exactly one hero module in a generated scene or production state.

### Void lamp

The brass lamp casts a hard circle of absence instead of light. The edge is a one-native-pixel teal rim. Papers crossing the edge are cleanly cropped, not softly darkened.

### Passive tentacle

One matte charcoal appendage rests through the barely open door. Its underside has restrained pale-teal sucker marks. It is passive, old, and bureaucratically unacknowledged—not lunging at the claimant.

### Board bleed

Two slow trails of dark viscous matter emerge from behind the noticeboard and pool on the linoleum. The forms remain perfectly squared and pinned.

### Occupied cabinet

The middle filing drawer bows outward in a shallow metal dome. Dark rounded pressure shapes with hairline teal veins press through the top gap. The event is patient, not explosive.

### Ethereal tube

One hard-banded translucent tube follows the wall/ceiling seam, passing into and out of solid architecture without connectors. Pale teal segments move inside it. It is a single infrastructure error, not a web of fantasy pipes.

### Suspended paper

Three forms hang between desk and floor in a held mid-fall pose while their shadows have already reached the linoleum.

## 12. Prompt assembly by asset type

Prompt Codex v2 used one global prompt for every image. That is replaced with scoped assemblies.

### 12.1 Environment plate

```text
[STYLE DNA]
+ [LOCKED CAMERA AND SINGLE-DESK COMPOSITION]
+ [CLEAN ROOM ANCHORS]
+ [LIGHTING MODE]
+ [ENVIRONMENT NEGATIVE]
```

Environment-only negative:

```text
people, claimant, human figure, portrait, framed photograph, second desk,
side table, second workstation, duplicate CRT, empty-room presentation,
generic isometric dollhouse, camera tilt, readable letters, soft rendering
```

“Empty-room presentation” prevents the isolated plate from being mistaken for the target final composition. The production plate itself still omits the claimant so that species can be swapped.

### 12.2 Claimant layer

```text
[STYLE DNA]
+ [SPECIES SILHOUETTE / MATERIAL / BEHAVIOUR]
+ seated across one office desk, fixed upper-centre gameplay footprint,
three-quarter frontal view, hands visible at shared desk edge,
sober bureaucratic clothing, upper-left fluorescent light
+ [CLAIMANT NEGATIVE]
```

Claimant negative:

```text
full room, second desk, wall portrait, ID card, hologram, floating bust,
human cosplay, extra random eyes, exposed fantasy armour, dramatic attack,
cropped hands, hidden face, readable text, smooth painterly rendering
```

### 12.3 Runtime anomaly layer

```text
[SELECT ONE HERO MODULE]
+ fixed room anchor and exact footprint
+ tier palette treatment
+ hard pixel bands, no bloom, no secondary anomaly
```

Anomaly references must be redrawn or masked into transparent production layers. ComfyUI transparency is not assumed to be correct.

```text

viewed from the claims worker's side of one continuous broad desk toward
one seated non-human claimant in the upper centre. The player and claimant
share opposite sides of the same desk. Back-left office door, left-wall
noticeboard, upper-right animated wall clock, far-right filing cabinet,
one fluorescent fixture above the processing axis. On the desk: wire inbox
upper-left, low CRT left of centre, clear current-form processing area,

crumpled form in the front-left discard lane, deliberate clear dead zone.
```

Append the pixel construction, material, lighting, and appropriate negative rules from this document. Do not request readable lettering.

This baseline is rejected if it introduces a second desk, removes the physical claimant, hides the claimant behind the CRT, loses the doorway, or turns the room into an isometric corner diorama.

## 14. ComfyUI execution contract

ComfyUI produces concept references, not finished sprites.

For every candidate, record:

- Workflow filename and revision.
- Checkpoint and text encoders.
- Positive and negative prompt files.
- Seed.
- Canvas and latent dimensions.
- Sampler, scheduler, steps, CFG/guidance, and denoise.
- Composition-control image and its provenance.
- ControlNet/IPAdapter model, preprocessor, strength, start, and end.
- Quantisation palette and colour count.
- Downscale and upscale method.
- Manual cleanup status.

Prompt length depends on the loaded encoder and workflow. Do not assume a universal 40-token or 77-token limit. Inspect the actual graph before compressing prose into weighted tags.

Use the approved baseline composition as the camera/layout control. Canny or depth strength `0.4–0.6` is an initial test range, not a fixed rule. Reject any setting that moves the claimant, desk silhouette, or room anchors.

Use one seed and one composition guide across the sanity ladder. Change only the tier treatment and selected hero module.

Palette transfer and nearest-neighbour scaling are intermediate treatments. They do not replace:

- manual cluster cleanup;
- silhouette correction;
- removal of generated microtexture;
- anatomy correction;
- layer separation;
- native-scale readability checks;
- artist redraw for final production.

## 15. Text and interface rule

Generated room and asset references contain no readable text. Use blank ruled forms, stamp-shaped blocks, and abstract CRT fields.

All real labels, claim text, clock numerals when required, CorpOS copy, and approval/rejection words are added through controlled production art or Unity UI. The negative `text, readable letters` therefore does not conflict with the gameplay requirement for readable information.

CorpOS is a separate paper-bureaucracy CRT interface layer. It must not be baked into the office plate and must not resemble glassmorphism, modern pill UI, or neon cyberpunk framing.

## 16. Artist delivery contract

- Layered `.aseprite`, `.psd`, or equivalent source with the layer names in section 11.
- Native 384 × 216 room and layer exports.
- Transparent claimant, prop, clock, and anomaly PNGs with no resampling.
- Native claimant sheets plus 4× nearest-neighbour review previews.
- Palette file and hex reference.
- Frame size, pivots, state names, timings, and loop/hold behaviour.
- Shared origin and registration marks for every room tier.
- No generated lettering.
- No flattened claimant baked into the final room plate.
- One contact sheet showing Clean, Uneasy, Contaminated, Impossible, and Fugue states from the same composition.

## 17. Unity handoff constraints

These requirements are recorded now so production art does not need to be rebuilt later:

- Texture Type: Sprite (2D and UI).
- Sprite Mode: Single or Multiple according to the supplied frame map.
- Filter Mode: Point.
- Compression: None.
- Generate Mip Maps: Off.
- Wrap Mode: Clamp.
- NPOT scaling: None.
- Consistent Pixels Per Unit within each asset family.
- Integer Canvas scaling and no fractional runtime transforms.
- Clock, claimant states, movable props, lighting, and anomalies remain independently addressable.
- Behavioural claimant swaps are driven by the client state machine, not manually keyed into the scene.

No Unity wiring begins until the art-lock verification gates below are approved.

## 18. Art-lock verification gates

### Gate A — composition

- [ ] One continuous desk reads immediately.
- [ ] The worker viewpoint and claimant-facing relationship are unmistakable.
- [ ] The claimant is seated physically across the desk.
- [ ] Door, noticeboard, clock, cabinet, and ceiling seam are visible.
- [ ] Claimant face and hands are unobstructed.
- [ ] No figurative desk photograph or wall claimant portrait exists.

### Gate B — pixel language

- [ ] The scene reads at 1× native resolution.
- [ ] Clusters are intentional and edges are crisp.
- [ ] Material ramps are shared and limited.
- [ ] No blur, soft bloom, painterly texture, or fake high-resolution detail remains.

### Gate C — production structure

- [ ] Environment, desk foreground, claimant, clock, props, light, and anomaly are separable.
- [ ] All species fit the same claimant registration footprint.
- [ ] All sanity tiers retain the same camera and room anchors.
- [ ] The clean baseline contains no anomaly colour.

### Gate D — controlled horror

- [ ] Each corrupted composition has one dominant anomaly.
- [ ] Supporting symptoms remain subordinate and tier-appropriate.
- [ ] Magenta is absent before Impossible and rare thereafter.
- [ ] Gameplay information stays readable under every distortion state.

## 19. Build order after approval

1. Produce the Clean Populated Baseline composition for visual validation.
2. Split/rebuild it as the clean environment plate plus shared claimant footprint.
3. Validate all four Pending claimants in that footprint.
4. Validate the Moth Accountant nine-state and fidget sheet.
5. Produce one hero anomaly as a separate layer and test the sanity ladder.
6. Complete equipment states and remaining claimant animations.
7. Import and wire the approved layers in Unity.
8. Capture the playable scene and compare it at native scale against the approved baseline.

No later step should compensate for a failure in an earlier gate.
