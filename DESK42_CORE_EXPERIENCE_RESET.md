# Desk 42 — Core Experience Reset

> Status: exploratory desk-centred direction. This has not been selected or
> superseded. See [DESK42_DIRECTION_EXPLORATION.md](DESK42_DIRECTION_EXPLORATION.md)
> for the current open set of candidate directions.

## Working hypothesis

This exploration proposed pausing production on modes, progression breadth, leaderboards, endings, vows, archetypes, hub content, and additional cards while the core direction remains unresolved.

Desk 42 currently has many systems surrounding a weak playable centre. The next milestone is not “more content.” It is one shift that proves the desk itself can produce observation, uncertainty, mastery, surprise, pressure, and expressive decisions.

## The promise

**You are trapped at a hostile bureaucratic desk, investigating impossible claims with unreliable tools. Every object is both a way to discover the truth and a temptation to manufacture it.**

The player should not feel like they are playing cards on top of a desk-themed interface. They should feel that they are operating a machine whose parts form a connected language:

- read the claimant;
- inspect and alter physical evidence;
- cross-check contradictory records;
- manipulate the claimant’s behaviour;
- decide which truth to file;
- live with the desk’s response.

## The current design failure

The implemented encounter has no real question for the player to answer.

- A claim displays text, identity, amount, species, tags, and NDA status, but none of that information is required to reach a ruling.
- Approve is currently treated as the correct resolution and Deny as the incorrect resolution. The game does not evaluate evidence, eligibility, contradictions, or a discovered ground truth.
- Punch cards mutate client state, but that state does not form a legible investigation that determines the ruling. Cards therefore become repeated effects rather than intelligent interventions.
- Modes, archetypes, vows, scoring, endings, and meta systems change the frame around the encounter more than they change the encounter itself.
- Fugue is described as an altered survival state, but the active loop treats zero sanity as an ending condition.
- Many desk objects create atmosphere, obstruction, or isolated gimmicks. They do not yet cooperate as one expressive toolset.

This is why the game feels like card spam despite its large feature inventory.

## Four experience pillars

### 1. Read before acting

Every case must contain a legible mystery. The player forms a hypothesis from documents, the claimant’s behaviour, audio, records, and anomalies. Information cannot be flavour-only.

### 2. Touch the bureaucracy

The player moves, marks, stamps, clips, files, calls, reveals, and destroys things. The physical actions are the verbs; cards modify those verbs or temporarily bend their rules.

### 3. Break rules deliberately

The joy of Balatro is not merely scoring. It is understanding a system well enough to violate its apparent limits. Desk 42 needs discoverable bureaucratic loopholes: combinations the player authors rather than bonuses the game quietly calculates.

### 4. Let the desk become the opponent

Pressure should transform the same trusted workspace. It should not merely add visual noise or randomise controls. Under stress, evidence becomes unreliable, tools develop agendas, and familiar procedures gain dangerous alternate meanings.

## The new case loop

Every claim follows six beats.

### 1. Receive

A case arrives through the pneumatic tube as a physical dossier. The player immediately sees:

- what is being claimed;
- the claimant’s visible state;
- the regulation currently governing the case;
- the deadline;
- the potential payout and personal consequence.

### 2. Inspect

The player can freely read the front-facing material, then spend limited time or sanity to investigate deeper:

- compare dates and signatures;
- study the claimant’s tells;
- reveal hidden ink;
- listen to an attached recording;
- search a restricted record;
- inspect a physical anomaly.

### 3. Cross-check

Evidence is moved between desk stations. Each station answers one kind of question and may introduce a new risk. The player chooses what not to verify; exhaustive checking should be safe but too slow.

### 4. Intervene

Punch cards change the claimant or the procedure long enough to create an opening:

- calm a hostile claimant so they reveal a statement;
- force a contradictory answer;
- suspend the clock while a form is being checked;
- redact a fact from the official record;
- make an invalid document temporarily admissible.

Cards are now tactical rule edits inside an investigation. They are not the investigation itself.

### 5. Rule

The final action is assembled physically. The player attaches selected evidence, chooses a resolution, and stamps the dossier:

- Approve;
- Deny;
- Hold for review;
- Reassign;
- or an unlocked illicit outcome.

The game evaluates the ruling against what the player actually established, not against a hard-coded moral label.

### 6. Consequence

The desk responds immediately and specifically:

- the claimant reacts;
- the payout cascade explains why the result changed;
- factions and supervisors respond;
- a chosen lie may become an official fact in a later case;
- damaged or altered documents remain on the desk;
- a successful loophole may be noticed and regulated tomorrow.

The next case should carry one visible scar from the previous decision.

## Every case has three truths

A claim is not simply valid or invalid.

| Truth | Question | Typical consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Procedural truth | Does the paperwork satisfy today’s regulation? | Salary, quota, supervisor heat |
| Human truth | What actually happened to this claimant? | Soul, relationships, future testimony |
| Anomalous truth | What impossible mechanism is operating underneath the claim? | Sanity, conspiracy progress, desk corruption |

The player rarely has enough time to prove all three. A strong case creates tension between them.

Example:

- The claimant really lost an arm in a workplace incident.
- Their form is invalid because the arm existed only retroactively.
- The company caused the temporal fault and will pay a bonus if the file is destroyed.

Approve, deny, hold, expose, or falsify can all be rational depending on the player’s build and intent.

## The minimum expressive desk

The vertical slice needs a small group of deeply connected tools, not dozens of shallow objects.

| Tool | Official use | Illicit or anomalous use | Primary cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Red pen | Mark a contradiction as evidence | Alter one field and make the alteration part of the official record | Supervisor heat |
| Desk lamp | Reveal watermarks and hidden text | Expose anomalous layers that damage sanity | Sanity |
| Telephone | Verify a witness, employer, or department | Coach a witness or call an entity that should not exist | Time / future consequence |
| Stapler | Bind evidence into a valid packet | Bind two facts so the system treats them as causally related | Limited staples / corruption |
| Punch-card machine | Temporarily alter client or procedure state | Stack rule edits into a loophole | Cards / machine heat |
| Stamp carousel | Commit the ruling | Access risky outcomes unlocked by the evidence packet | Irreversible commitment |

Each tool must affect at least two other systems. If an object only plays an animation, opens a panel, or grants a flat bonus, it is not yet part of the core desk.

## Fugue should be playable

Fugue is not a red health bar reaching zero. It is the desk ceasing to agree with itself.

During fugue:

- the claimant’s spoken statement and transcript diverge;
- duplicate documents appear, but only one casts a shadow;
- the clock counts toward two different deadlines;
- a stamp changes the wording of the regulation it is meant to enforce;
- the telephone connects to a previous claimant;
- the player can sacrifice an established fact to restore one reliable desk object.

The design rule is crucial: **fugue may corrupt information, but it must not steal input or make outcomes arbitrary.** The player survives by learning the desk’s physical tells and choosing which reality to preserve.

## The compelling run loop

Engagement should come from dense learning and authored surprise, not from padding.

- **Action loop:** notice → hypothesise → risk a tool → receive a tactile answer.
- **Case loop:** investigate → construct a ruling → see a clear cascade → inherit a consequence.
- **Shift loop:** exploit today’s regulations → trigger a counter-regulation → adapt the desk build.
- **Run loop:** discover a bureaucratic engine → push it until it becomes unstable → decide whether to profit, expose it, or survive it.

The player should regularly say:

- “I think this document is lying.”
- “If I staple these together, the system might accept both.”
- “I can make more money if I preserve this contradiction.”
- “That loophole worked, but now the next regulation is targeting it.”
- “The desk changed, but I know which version is real.”

## Vertical slice: one excellent shift

Build only enough content to prove the loop:

- one 20–25 minute shift;
- six authored cases with controlled procedural variants;
- three case families;
- five connected desk tools;
- twelve punch cards that modify investigation verbs;
- four regulations, with no more than three active at once;
- one returning claimant whose second case reflects the first ruling;
- one mid-shift policy change;
- one fugue sequence that can be survived;
- one personal expense creating a real moral trade-off;
- one final supervisor review that reflects the evidence trail.

No new mode is allowed to enter the slice. Daily Brief and Unpaid Overtime can later reuse this loop, but they cannot substitute for it.

## Content to freeze

Keep the existing work in the repository, but stop designing or polishing:

- leaderboards;
- Daily Brief;
- endless mode;
- additional archetypes;
- additional vows;
- additional endings;
- additional progression currencies;
- hub expansion;
- conspiracy-board breadth;
- more generic cards;
- cosmetic rewards.

These systems become valuable only after a single case is pleasurable without them.

## Player-readiness gates

The slice is not ready because it contains all planned features. It is ready when playtests show:

1. Players read documents without being ordered to.
2. Players form and revise a hypothesis about a case.
3. Players use several desk objects because the objects answer different questions.
4. Players can explain why their ruling succeeded, failed, or created a consequence.
5. Players discover at least one combination that feels like their own clever exploit.
6. Players make a ruling they know is procedurally wrong for a reason they care about.
7. Players want to replay the shift to test a different line of reasoning.
8. The first ten minutes are enjoyable with all meta-progression hidden.

## Immediate execution order

1. Replace binary “Approve is correct” resolution with evidence-based case truth and multiple rulings.
2. Author two complete cases on paper before generating more procedural content.
3. Implement the red pen, lamp, telephone, stapler, and punch-card machine as one connected investigation grammar.
4. Make cards modify desk actions and temporary rules rather than act as the dominant verb.
5. Build a readable resolution cascade that shows facts, rule matches, lies, costs, and consequences.
6. Turn zero sanity into the playable fugue transformation.
7. Test the six-case shift before restoring any frozen system.

## North-star test

Remove the deck, progression, leaderboards, endings, and lore.

If processing one claim with the desk is still absorbing, Desk 42 has a game.

If it is not, nothing surrounding it should be expanded.
